Audience Research

The selected target audience for this game is young teenagers aged 11-16 years old. This demographic was chosen because individuals within this age range demonstrate high levels of digital engagement while simultaneously beginning to develop independent online behaviours, making cybersecurity education particularly relevant.

Young people between 11–16 years old are among the most active users of digital technologies. According to Ofcom’s *Children and Parents: Media Use and Attitudes Report (2023)*, approximately 98% of children aged 12–15 own or have access to smartphones and use online platforms regularly throughout daily life. This high level of digital interaction increases exposure to phishing attempts, suspicious links, social engineering techniques, and unsafe online behaviours.

Gaming behaviours further support this audience selection. Ofcom reported that approximately 87% of individuals aged 12–15 participate in gaming activities, with a large proportion engaging with online games and communicating with others while playing. Since gaming already forms a significant part of entertainment consumption for this demographic, delivering cybersecurity education through interactive gameplay provides a more familiar and engaging learning environment compared to traditional awareness methods.

The decision to focus on early adolescents is also supported by cybersecurity education research. Developing safe online behaviours during adolescence is important because this age group increasingly makes independent decisions online without parental supervision. Educational studies examining game-based phishing awareness have shown improvements in recognising suspicious content when learners actively participate in realistic scenarios rather than passively consuming information.

The game’s design choices were developed specifically around the characteristics of this demographic. The retro-inspired pixel art aesthetic uses simplified visuals to reduce cognitive overload while maintaining engagement. The increasing difficulty progression supports younger players by introducing basic concepts before gradually presenting more complex cybersecurity challenges. Additionally, the pressure-based mechanics encourage rapid decision making, reflecting the real-world situations users may encounter when interacting with suspicious online content.


Overall, the selected audience aligns strongly with both the educational objectives and gameplay mechanics of the project, making this demographic suitable for delivering cybersecurity awareness through interactive game-based learning.
